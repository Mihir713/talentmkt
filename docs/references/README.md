# Reference study

Captured with Playwright at 1440×900 on 2026-10-02. Patterns only: we take layout, hierarchy,
ticket flow and how rules are explained. We take no brand, assets or copy.

**Manifold** (`manifold-home.png`, `manifold-market*.png`). The closest model: play money and
an AMM. Taking: the probability stated as a single large figure with its change next to it, a
range toggle (1H to ALL) sitting on the chart's top edge, and tabs for trades and holders under
the market. Not taking: the side-by-side "Bet YES / Bet NO" slab buttons (they read as a
binary game, and they hide price, payout and fee until after the click), the comment feed, and
the marketing accordions in the right rail.

**Kalshi** (`kalshi-home.png`, `kalshi-market*.png`). Taking: the trade ticket as a fixed right
rail with Buy/Sell as tabs, a YES/NO segmented control that carries the current price on each
side, one amount field, and the result spelled out underneath ("Odds", "Max payout"). The chart
labels its line ends directly instead of using a legend, which is the data-desk move we want.
Not taking: the sports scoreboard chrome, condensed display type, green-on-white brand, and the
long multi-market lists below the chart (one market per page here).

**Polymarket** (`polymarket-home.png`, `polymarket-market*.png`). Taking: quick-amount chips
(+10, +50) beside the amount, and showing "Yes 56.6¢ / No 43.5¢" so the price of each side is
visible before choosing. Not taking: red/green YES/NO (not colorblind-safe and reads as
gain/loss), candidate photos, the blue brand CTA that competes with the side colors, and the
dense category strip.

**Linear** (`linear-home*.png`). Taking: density without clutter, small but legible UI type,
icons in one stroke weight, hairline separation instead of boxes, quiet chrome that lets the
content carry color. Not taking: the near-black marketing world and its glow, or the issue-
tracker information architecture.

**Stripe docs** (`stripe-docs*.png`). The model for the rules box and the "What we store"
explainer. Taking: a plain heading, one-sentence summary under it, then short paragraphs
with defined terms; an "On this page" rail for long explanations; and stating exactly what
happens rather than what is "supported". Not taking: the documentation chrome, link density, or
the purple link color.
